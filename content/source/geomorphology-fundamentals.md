# Fundamentals of Geomorphology

**Course:** Earth Science basics · **Lesson 1** · about 12 minutes · board videos + check questions
**Audience:** school students (class 8–12) and curious adults; no science background needed
**Teaching languages:** Telugu, Hindi, English (technical terms stay in English, as in Indian textbooks)

## Learning goals
By the end, the learner can:
1. Say what geomorphology studies.
2. Tell **endogenic** (inside-the-Earth) forces from **exogenic** (surface) forces.
3. Explain the chain **weathering → erosion → transport → deposition**.
4. Name the five agents that shape land and one landform each makes.
5. Describe how plate movement builds mountains and how landforms age (the geomorphic cycle).

---

## 1. What is geomorphology?
*Geo* = Earth, *morph* = shape, *logy* = study. **Geomorphology is the study of landforms** (mountains, valleys, plains, plateaus, deltas, dunes, caves) **and of the processes that make and change them.**

Land looks fixed, but it is always changing, mostly too slowly for us to notice in one lifetime. Two groups of forces compete:

| | Endogenic forces | Exogenic forces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where | Inside the Earth | On the surface |
| Energy | Earth's internal heat | The Sun, gravity, water, wind, ice |
| Effect | **Build up** land: raise, fold, break, add new rock | **Wear down** land: break, carry away, deposit |
| Fast example | Earthquakes, volcanic eruptions | Landslides, flash floods |
| Slow example | Mountain building (Himalayas still rising) | A river cutting a valley over millions of years |

**Key idea:** every landform is the result of this tug-of-war. Endogenic forces push land up; exogenic forces bring it down.

**Check:** Is a volcano formed by an endogenic or an exogenic force? → *Endogenic.*

## 2. Weathering, erosion, deposition
These words are often mixed up. They are steps in one chain:

1. **Weathering:** rock breaks down **where it is**. Nothing moves yet.
   - *Physical:* heating by day and cooling by night cracks rock; water freezes in cracks and splits them.
   - *Chemical:* rainwater (slightly acidic) dissolves or changes minerals, like limestone dissolving.
   - *Biological:* plant roots grow into cracks; burrowing animals.
2. **Mass movement:** gravity pulls loose material downhill (landslides, rock falls), common on the Western Ghats in the monsoon.
3. **Erosion and transport:** an agent (water, wind, ice, waves) **picks up and carries** the broken material.
4. **Deposition:** when the agent slows down, it **drops** the material, building new land (a delta, a sand dune).

**Memory line:** *Break → move → drop.*

**Check:** A rock cracks in the desert heat but stays in place. Is that weathering or erosion? → *Weathering.*

## 3. The five agents and their landforms
| Agent | What it cuts (erosion) | What it builds (deposition) | Indian example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Running water** (rivers) | V-shaped valleys, gorges, waterfalls | Meanders, ox-bow lakes, floodplains, **deltas** | Godavari & Krishna deltas; Papikondalu gorge on the Godavari |
| **Wind** (deserts) | Mushroom rocks, hollows | **Sand dunes** (barchans) | Thar Desert, Rajasthan |
| **Glaciers** (moving ice) | **U-shaped valleys**, cirques | Moraines (piles of rock) | Gangotri glacier, Himalayas |
| **Sea waves** | Cliffs, caves, sea arches | Beaches, sandbars | Rocky stretches of the east and west coasts |
| **Groundwater** (in limestone) | **Caves** | Stalactites (hang down), stalagmites (grow up) | **Borra Caves** (Araku, AP), **Belum Caves** (Kurnool, AP) |

**Tip:** V-valley = river, U-valley = glacier.

**Check:** Which agent makes a U-shaped valley? → *A glacier.*

## 4. Plates, mountains and the geomorphic cycle
**Plate tectonics.** The Earth's outer shell is broken into huge **plates** that move a few centimetres a year.
- Plates **push together** → rock folds up into **fold mountains**. The Indian plate pushing into the Eurasian plate is still raising the **Himalayas**.
- Plates **pull apart** or crack → blocks drop down and form **rift valleys**. The **Narmada** flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges.
- Plate edges also bring **earthquakes** and **volcanoes**.

**The geomorphic cycle** (W. M. Davis). A raised landscape wears down in stages:
1. **Youth:** steep slopes, deep V-valleys, waterfalls, fast rivers.
2. **Maturity:** wider valleys, rounded hills, rivers start to meander.
3. **Old age:** a low, almost flat plain (*peneplain*), slow meandering rivers, ox-bow lakes.

The lowest level a river can erode down to is its **base level**, usually sea level.

**Check:** A river with waterfalls and a deep V-valley is in which stage? → *Youth.*

## Summary
- Geomorphology = landforms + the processes that shape them.
- Endogenic forces build up; exogenic forces wear down.
- Break (weathering) → move (erosion/transport) → drop (deposition).
- Rivers, wind, glaciers, waves and groundwater each leave their own landforms.
- Moving plates build mountains and rift valleys; landscapes age from youth to old age.
