# Life Processes (AP Board, Class 10 Biology)

## What are life processes?

Living things must keep doing certain jobs to stay alive, even while they sleep. These jobs are called life processes. The main life processes are nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion. All of them need energy, and that energy comes from food.

## Nutrition

Nutrition is how living things get their food. Green plants make their own food: this is called autotrophic nutrition. Animals and human beings depend on other living things for food: this is called heterotrophic nutrition.

## Photosynthesis

Green plants make food by photosynthesis. In the leaves, a green pigment called chlorophyll absorbs sunlight. Using this energy, the plant takes in carbon dioxide from the air through tiny pores in the leaves called stomata, and water from the soil through its roots. It makes glucose and releases oxygen. In short: carbon dioxide and water, with sunlight and chlorophyll, give glucose and oxygen.

## Nutrition in human beings

Digestion starts in the mouth. Teeth break the food into small pieces, and saliva contains an enzyme called salivary amylase that breaks starch into sugar. Food moves down the food pipe, the oesophagus, to the stomach. In the stomach, hydrochloric acid and the enzyme pepsin start digesting proteins. In the small intestine, bile from the liver and juices from the pancreas complete digestion. Digested food is absorbed into the blood through tiny finger-like villi. The large intestine absorbs extra water, and the waste leaves the body through the anus.

## Respiration

Respiration releases energy from food. Inside our cells, glucose is broken down. With oxygen, this is called aerobic respiration: it gives carbon dioxide, water and a lot of energy, and it happens in the mitochondria. Without oxygen, this is called anaerobic respiration: yeast makes alcohol and carbon dioxide, and our muscles make lactic acid during hard exercise, which causes cramps. The energy is stored in the cell as ATP.

## Breathing in human beings

Air enters through the nostrils, passes the throat and the windpipe, called the trachea, and goes into the bronchi and the lungs. The lungs have millions of tiny air sacs called alveoli. There, oxygen passes into the blood and carbon dioxide passes out of it. Haemoglobin in the red blood cells carries the oxygen around the body.

## Transportation in human beings

Transportation carries food, oxygen and wastes around the body. In human beings, the heart pumps blood. The heart has four chambers. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins bring blood back to the heart, and tiny capillaries connect them. Blood rich in oxygen and blood poor in oxygen are kept separate: this is called double circulation. Platelets help the blood to clot at a cut. Lymph also carries digested fats and helps fight infection.

## Transportation in plants

Plants have two transport tissues. Xylem carries water and minerals from the roots up to the leaves. Water lost from the leaves, called transpiration, helps pull the water up. Phloem carries the food made in the leaves to all parts of the plant. This is called translocation.

## Excretion in human beings

Excretion removes harmful wastes made in the body. In human beings, the two kidneys filter the blood. Each kidney has about a million tiny filtering units called nephrons. Useful substances like glucose and water are taken back into the blood, and the waste becomes urine. Urine goes through the ureters to the urinary bladder and leaves the body through the urethra. If the kidneys fail, a dialysis machine can clean the blood.

## Excretion in plants

Plants lose extra water through transpiration, and release oxygen through the stomata. Some wastes are stored in old leaves that later fall off, or in the bark, as gums and resins.
